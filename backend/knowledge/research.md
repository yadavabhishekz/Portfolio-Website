## How I learn: reading research papers and trying them on real data
Research and continuous learning are a big part of how I work. I like reading a paper and then trying to get the idea working on real data. That's usually where I learn the most. I also keep going back to the fundamentals, since the frameworks change every few months but the math underneath doesn't.

## My research interests
Visual representation learning, self-supervised learning, one-shot recognition, domain adaptation, local feature matching, image retrieval, model compression, and image restoration. I read computer vision and deep learning papers to find techniques that can actually improve real systems, and to understand new feature extraction models and how they could fit into existing recognition pipelines.

## How I decide whether a research idea is worth adopting
I don't implement a paper just because of its reported results. First I check whether the method suits the characteristics of my own data, because a technique that shines on a benchmark can struggle on messy real-world images. Then I weigh the expected accuracy improvement against implementation complexity, computational overhead, and inference latency. My approach is to combine ideas from research with real dataset analysis and practical experimentation.
