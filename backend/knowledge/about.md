## Who I am
I'm Abhishek Yadav, an AI Engineer based in Delhi, India. Since September 2026 I've been working full-time as a Computer Vision Engineer at Spraxa Solutions, on object detection, instance segmentation, visual recognition, and model optimization. Outside work, my projects focus on Generative AI: LLMs, RAG, NLP, AI agents, and shipping production-grade AI applications, not just notebook demos. I graduated with a B.Tech in Computer Engineering from Bharati Vidyapeeth (Deemed to be University) College of Engineering, Pune, Maharashtra (2022 – 2026).

## Where I'm based
I'm based in Delhi, India, where I live and work. I studied in Pune, Maharashtra.

## My journey into AI
I started with classic machine learning: linear and logistic regression, random forests, SVMs, and clustering. Then I moved into deep learning: CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, Transformers, and attention. From there I went deep into Generative AI, building with Groq, OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, Llama, Hugging Face, LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, and vector databases. Professionally I now work in computer vision, so I cover both sides: vision models and LLM systems.

## What I care about
I like building AI systems that solve a real problem for real users, not AI for AI's sake. If I can't explain why something matters to an actual person, I don't think it's worth building.

## Hobbies, interests, and what I do for fun outside work
In my free time I like keeping my brain busy. My hobbies:
- Chess: I love the strategy of it, thinking a few moves ahead and weighing trade-offs.
- Solving Rubik's cubes: it's basically pattern recognition plus algorithms, so it scratches the same itch as AI work.
- Mental math on Matiks, a competitive mental-math platform with live one-on-one math duels and puzzles. I play it sometimes to keep my number-crunching sharp.
- Cooking: I enjoy experimenting in the kitchen; tweaking a recipe until it comes out right feels a lot like tuning a model.
- Reading AI and computer vision research papers, and trying out new AI tools as soon as they come out. Part curiosity, part habit.

## How my hobbies connect to my work
Almost every hobby of mine is a puzzle with a feedback loop. Chess is about planning ahead and evaluating options, cubes are about finding the right algorithm for a pattern, mental math is about speed and accuracy under pressure, and cooking is experiment, taste, adjust, repeat. That's the same loop I follow when building and debugging AI systems.
